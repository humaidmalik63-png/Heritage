How it works

Image to evidence, in four moves.

A photograph becomes a vector, a vector becomes a match, and a match becomes an answer you can check.

  1. 01
    Image

    You provide a photograph of a monument.

  2. 02
    AI Analysis

    A CLIP vision model converts it into a 512-dimensional vector.

  3. 03
    Heritage Match

    Vector search retrieves the closest known monuments and scores confidence.

  4. 04
    Evidence

    A grounded answer is assembled from cited historical sources.

The Collection

Sites held in the index.

Five monuments, spanning two millennia of South Asian history, each one photographed and documented against its own record.

Mughal Empire, 17th century

Taj Mahal

A mausoleum built entirely for memory.

Commissioned in 1632 by Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal, and widely regarded as the finest expression of Mughal architecture.

Agra, Uttar Pradesh
Delhi Sultanate, 12th–13th century

Qutb Minar

The first tower of a city that was never finished.

A 72.5 metre minaret begun by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, marking the opening of the Delhi Sultanate and its earliest garden city.

Delhi, Delhi
Mughal Empire, 17th century

Red Fort

Power held behind sandstone walls.

The primary residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly two centuries, and the site of India’s annual Independence Day ceremony.

Delhi, Delhi
2nd century BCE – 6th century CE

Ajanta Caves

Painted in the dark, two thousand years ago.

Thirty rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments carved into a horseshoe-shaped gorge, celebrated for their wall paintings and sculptures.

Aurangabad, Maharashtra
Eastern Ganga Dynasty, 13th century

Sun Temple, Konark

A temple built as the chariot of the sun.

A thirteenth-century monument shaped like the chariot of Surya, with twelve pairs of carved stone wheels and a carved narrative across every surface.

Konark, Odisha
